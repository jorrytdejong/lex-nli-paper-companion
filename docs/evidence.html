Why is recall higher than precision?

The top five models predict about 1.4 relations per LEX set, while references average 1.0. They also produce fewer empty sets than the references (15% versus 29%). Extra relations can find more of what is needed while lowering precision. See the paper's Section 7.1.

02 · The reference answers

How were human explanations made?

The authors curated 280 SNLI and 82 SICK entailments. Each problem received three independent LEX annotations, followed by adjudication when the explanations differed.

  1. 1 · Select the inferenceThe final set has 362 problems. Some need no added lexical relation: 29% of reference LEX sets are empty.
  2. 2 · Annotate independentlyThree annotators each identify the lexical relations needed to explain the entailment.
  3. 3 · Review differencesAgreement or adjudication yields one accepted LEX set for 223 problems. The remaining 139 retain multiple valid reference sets; 22 problems receive a fourth alternative during adjudication.

03 · Worked examples

What does a LEX actually explain?

One inference, two lexical bridges

Premise A zoo keeper holds a chinchilla.

Hypothesis A person holds a small animal.

zoo keeper ⊑ person explains the person.
chinchilla ⊑ small animal explains the animal.

LEX makes the needed word-level knowledge explicit. It supplies part of the explanation; LangPro still decides whether a formal proof follows.

Several references can be valid

Premise A woman observes a rusted antique car.

Hypothesis A woman looks at an old car.

Two of the accepted reference variants are:

Variant A

antique ⊑ old
observe ⊑ look

Variant B

antique car ⊑ old car
observe ⊑ look at

This paper example has four accepted combinations. They differ in how much of the phrase each relation covers, which is why scoring against one answer alone would be too strict.

04 · Interpreting proofs

When can a successful proof mislead?

On a separate set of 1,000 SNLI entailments, LangPro proves 157 with WordNet alone. The best agentic LLM plus WordNet reaches 212. Those 55 extra proofs measure coverage, not the soundness of every proposed relation.

The skier case

Premise Two skiers stand near a snowy mountain.

Hypothesis Some humans standing.

A reasonable relation is suppliedskier ⊑ human still does not let LangPro prove the entailment.
A parser mismatch blocks the proofThe pipeline treats “standing” in the hypothesis as a noun, while the premise uses “stand” as a verb.
The agentic revision unlocks a proofThe model adds stand ⊑ standing and standing ⊑ stand. The eventual proof uses stand ⊑ standing with skier ⊑ human.

The new relation bridges an error in the analysis pipeline. The proof succeeds, but that does not establish that the added relation is good lexical knowledge for explaining the inference.

The paper's manual analysis of additional Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite proofs found that only five of the examined problems included an added relation judged semantically reasonable in isolation. Other gains often compensated for parser or prover weaknesses. Paper Section 8.2

What other mistakes did the authors find?

In their error analysis, models sometimes repeat inflectional variants of one relation, or choose a whole phrase where the task expects smaller lexical relations. The authors also found valid predictions missing from the human references. These issues matter when interpreting an exact score as a complete measure of explanation quality. See Section 8.1.
