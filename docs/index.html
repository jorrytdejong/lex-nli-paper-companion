Explaining Textual Entailment with Lexical Entailments

Using LLMs to Supply Lexical Relations for Formal Proofs

We ask language models to explain why one sentence follows from another using small, explicit lexical relations. Then we test whether those relations help a formal reasoning system.

Jorryt de Jong · Stefan Morača · Ettore Cesari · Lasha Abzianidze
Utrecht University

See the findings Read the paper

Explore the evidence → Model results, annotation decisions, worked examples, and proof cases.

The short answer

LLMs can add useful lexical knowledge to a symbolic prover. Their explanations still overgenerate, and more successful proofs do not always mean better knowledge.

01 · The task

Make the hidden lexical step visible

Natural language inference (NLI) asks whether a hypothesis follows from a premise. A correct entailment label does not show which word-level knowledge supports it.

Premise A zoo keeper holds a chinchilla.

Hypothesis A person holds a small animal.

zoo keeper⊑person
chinchilla⊑small animal

LEX is a set of directed entailment relations: the left expression is at least as specific as the right. These relations capture the lexical part of an explanation, not a complete proof.

02 · The study

One benchmark, two evaluations

The authors curated entailment examples from SNLI and SICK, keeping multiple valid explanations when annotators disagreed.

362entailment problems
1–4reference LEX sets per problem
1,108reference LEX sets
  1. ExplainModels predict an NLI label and a structured LEX set.
  2. ComparePredicted relations are scored against human-curated alternatives.
  3. ProveRelations are added to LangPro’s lexical knowledge base; the prover attempts a formal proof.

Two questions, two kinds of evidence

Intrinsic: does the explanation match references? Extrinsic: can the prover use it?

The model proposes relations and revises them once. In this setup, LangPro proves 212 problems; proof success alone does not establish that every supplied relation is sound.

Read the proof count carefully. Some extra relations repair parser or prover mismatches, and some are semantically unsound. More proofs are not the same as more faithful explanations.

04 · Scope and caveats

What this study does—and does not—claim

The work studies structured lexical explanations for entailment examples. It does not yet cover contradiction and neutral cases, or capture every part of NLI reasoning.

  • Multiple LEX sets can be valid for the same inference.
  • LLMs often add extra or overly broad relations.
  • Proof coverage measures what LangPro can prove; it is not a direct measure of explanation soundness.
What is a lexical entailment?

A directed relation from a specific expression to a more general or equivalent one, such as guitar ⊑ musical instrument. Multiword expressions are allowed when the words alone would not preserve the meaning.

Why keep several human references?

Annotators can give different but valid lexical explanations. The benchmark preserves up to four reference LEX sets for a problem rather than forcing one wording or one explanation.
